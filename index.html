De-escalation in Ministry

Equipping our church to be a safe, compassionate, and welcoming space for all.

Welcome and Purpose

This training outlines principles and techniques to compassionately and safely de-escalate situations with individuals who may be disruptive due to distress, intoxication, or mental health challenges. Our goal is to maintain a peaceful environment while showing Christ's love to those in need, relying on the 1+3 Step Method.

Understanding the Signs and Stages of Agitation

The goal is to spot the early warning signs and start de-escalation as early as possible, before the person has an opportunity to elevate to a higher level[cite: 6]. Everybody eventually comes down, but the way we treat the person has a lot of bearing on how high they go, and how soon they come down[cite: 6].

  • 1. The Three Triggers

    There are three main sources of emotional escalation: The person feels unsafe, the person feels disrespected, or the person feels that the organization doesn't care about them[cite: 9].

  • 2. The Brain's Response

    When fear and anger dominate, the pre-frontal cortex shuts down and a person can lose control over their emotional responses[cite: 9]. The limbic "flight, fight or freeze" part of the brain takes over, meaning the highly agitated person is unable to effectively resolve conflict[cite: 9].

  • 3. Spotting the Signs

    Watch for vocal shifts. A normal tendency is to have a high pitched, tight voice when scared or nervous[cite: 6]. Also watch for physical signs of being angry, frustrated, or upset[cite: 9].

The 1+3 Step Method

1. Project Calm (You)

Your calm presence is contagious. Take a deep breath, speak slowly in a low tone, and maintain a neutral, compassionate expression. DO NOT smile, as this can look like mockery to an agitated person.

+1. Validate Emotion

Allow them to vent without interrupting. Validate their emotions ("I can see that you are very upset") without necessarily agreeing with their actions.

+2. Help Find Options

Help them explore safe, pastoral choices rather than transactional fixes. Suggest moving to a quiet space, getting a glass of water, or arranging a time to talk.

+3. Allow for Choice

An agitated person feels trapped. Empower them by giving them the final say between two positive choices to help them regain control.

The Stealth Protocol (Mid-Service)

When a disruption occurs during a church service, the goal shifts from fully resolving the issue to quietly relocating the distressed individual without drawing the congregation's attention.

  • Contact and Cover: Never engage alone. One volunteer (Contact) gently engages the person, while a second (Cover) stays back to observe and manage the environment.
  • Line-of-Sight Signaling: The "Cover" volunteer should use pre-arranged eye contact or subtle hand signals with a designated leader to request backup silently.
  • The Sound Desk Command Post: Utilize the AV/Sound desk as a silent monitoring station. Because they have a clear view of the room, they can quietly call for pastoral backup or 111 if needed.

Scripting the Response

  • Instead of trying to "fix" the problem, ask:

    "You seem to be in distress. Is there anything I can do to help you right now?"

  • Instead of arguing logic, offer a private space:

    "It seems like a difficult moment. Would there be a more private space where we could go to get a glass of water and talk quietly?"

  • Give empowering choices for later resolution:

    "Would it be helpful to talk with the vicar after this service, or perhaps we could make an appointment for you to raise your concerns later in the week when you are feeling calmer?"

When De-escalation Fails

Critical Rule: Never physically intervene.

If a situation escalates to a physical threat or a fight breaks out, your pastoral role ends. Follow these steps immediately:

  1. Signal for Help: Discreetly get the attention of another team member or leader immediately.
  2. Create Distance: Step back. Increase the space between you and the individual. Do not step between fighting people.
  3. Isolate the Area: Focus entirely on guiding bystanders and congregation members away from the immediate area.
  4. Call 111: If there is any threat of violence or immediate danger, call emergency services without hesitation.
